# Living with Temutom – Concept Design

*16 November 2026 · Concept 11 of 11 · [Back to concept list](../game-concepts.md)*

A companion to [Temutom: Staff Management](10-temutom.md). That game is played as the cat. This one is played as the person who lives with him.

All numbers in this document are proposed starting values, to be tuned through playtesting.

## 1. Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pitch** | You live with an orange cat who believes you exist to serve him. Get through your day, keep him satisfied, and keep your thumb away from his teeth. When you get it wrong, he takes it out on your screen. |
| **Genre** | Reverse virtual pet with light real-time action |
| **Audience** | Cat owners and anyone who follows cat humour online; teens and adults |
| **Orientation** | Portrait, one thumb |
| **Session** | One "moment" of 60 to 90 seconds; progress saves after every moment |
| **Works offline** | Yes |

## 2. The idea

In an ordinary virtual pet game the pet needs the player. Temutom does not need the player. He employs the player.

The view is the player's own: a kitchen worktop, a desk, a sofa, seen in first person. There is no hand drawn on screen. **The player's thumb is the hand**, and the glass of the phone is the only thing between it and the cat.

Temutom knows the glass is there. When he is displeased he does not attack a character in the game; he attacks the screen:

- He **swipes** at the thumb and leaves claw marks across the glass, over the scene and the interface alike.
- He **bites**, and the whole screen floods a deep red.
- He **stares** from so close that his face fills the display.
- He **sits on** the buttons the player needs.
- He **knocks** parts of the interface off the bottom of the screen.

## 3. What carries over, and what is turned round

| In *Staff Management* (as the cat) | In *Living with Temutom* (as the human) |
|---|---|
| The Attention Band is a gauge on screen | The band is **hidden**. The player reads it from his body. |
| The player chooses the five verbs | He uses the five verbs **on the player and on the screen** |
| Staff misread his demands | **The player** has to work out what he wants |
| The player trains the staff's Compliance | He trains the player; his opinion of them is their **Standing** |
| Staff Morale limits his aggression | **Plasters** limit how many injuries the player can take |
| The Brain Cell enables his Schemes | The player must **spot** the Brain Cell arriving and decide whether to stop him |
| His lapses raise staff morale | His lapses are the player's breathing space, and their reward |
| He reviews the staff | He reviews **the player** |

His character and voice are unchanged. He is never wrong, and nothing is ever his fault.

## 4. Core mechanic: reading the cat

Temutom still has a narrow, drifting zone of acceptable attention. The player cannot see it. They see him.

| What he is doing | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Stares; follows the thumb with his eyes; walks across the work | Too little attention | Acknowledge him, stroke him |
| Purrs; half-closed eyes; slow blink; kneading | Just right | Carry on exactly as you are |
| Tail tip flicks; ears turn sideways; purr stops | Slightly too much | Ease off |
| Tail lashes; skin ripples; ears flat; pupils wide | **Warning** | Take your thumb away, now |
| Strike | Too late | – |

The band moves. Stroking that was welcome five seconds ago is assault now, and he gives no notice of the change beyond his tail.

**Where he is touched matters.** The head, cheeks and chin are safe. The back is acceptable for a while. The tail and paws are not. The belly, which he will display invitingly, is a trap.

**The fairness rule:** he always shows the warning before he strikes, and a player who lifts their thumb in time is not hurt. The claws still mark the glass.

## 5. Core gameplay

1. A moment begins in one place in the home, with a small **chore** to do: make the coffee, write the email, fold the washing.
2. Chores are done by holding the thumb on each highlighted item in turn.
3. Temutom arrives. He wants something, or he wants attention, or he wants the player to stop.
4. The player has one thumb. It can be on the chore or on the cat, never both.
5. The moment ends after 60 to 90 seconds with a score, one to three stars, and his written review of the player.

The whole game is that division of one thumb between what the player needs to do and what he requires.

## 6. His demands

He does not say what he wants. He makes it known.

| Demand | How the player meets it |
|---|---|
| **Food** | Drag a pouch to the bowl |
| **Door** | Swipe the door open. He may then sit in the doorway and consider it. |
| **Lap** | Stop, and hold still while he settles. The chore must then be done around him. |
| **Play** | Drag the wand toy for him to hunt |
| **Nothing** | He wants nothing. He is merely screaming. Acknowledge him. |

The clues are where he stands, what he looks at and the time of day. A wrong guess annoys him. *"No."*

**If a demand is not met, he escalates**, using the same five verbs as in the first game:

1. **Stare.** He sits and looks. Then he comes closer. Then his face fills the screen.
2. **Scream.** The screen shakes, and for a moment every label on the interface reads "NOW".
3. **Knock Off.** First an ornament. Then the mug. Then the score counter, which drops off the bottom of the screen and has to be picked up.
4. **Sit On.** He settles on the chore, or on the buttons.
5. **Smack.** See below.

## 7. What he does to the screen

### Claw marks

When he swipes, four parallel slashes follow the path of his paw across the display, on top of everything, including the interface. Fresh marks are bright and sharp; they dull and fade over about twenty seconds. Several can build up, and they make the scene harder to read.

If the thumb was under the paw, the player loses a plaster.

### The bite

When he bites, a deep red floods outward from the point of the bite until it covers the screen, holds for a moment, then drains away. The phone gives one hard pulse. The player cannot act for a second and a half, and loses a plaster.

It is stylised: a wash of colour, with no wound shown. A setting softens it for players sensitive to flashing.

### Plasters

The player starts each moment with three plasters. When they are gone, the moment ends. *"Go and run that under the tap."* One tap starts again.

### The rest

- **The close stare:** his face blocks the screen until he is acknowledged with a tap. Holding his gaze too long is a challenge, and he accepts challenges.
- **Sitting on things:** he can be lured off with a treat, waited out, or lifted. Lifting is quick and he resents it.
- **The head-butt:** rarely, when everything is right, he bumps the screen with his forehead. The display nudges, the phone gives a soft thump, and nothing is said about it.
- **The slow blink:** if he slow-blinks and the player answers by resting their thumb gently on an empty part of the screen, he is calmer for a while.

## 8. The Brain Cell

All orange cats share one brain cell. The player can tell when it is Temutom's turn: his eyes, which normally point in very slightly different directions, line up.

**When he has it,** he schemes. He opens the treat cupboard. He works the door handle. He walks across the keyboard and undoes the last step of the chore. Each Scheme takes a few visible seconds, and the player can stop it with a tap, or let him get on with it and deal with the result.

**When he does not,** he lapses. He pounces on the thumb as though it were a beetle, harmlessly. He misjudges the jump to the worktop. He gets into a box. He forgets, mid-scream, what he wanted, and the demand goes away.

Lapses cost the player nothing and are the calm in each moment. Tapping him during one counts as having seen it. *"You saw nothing."*

## 9. One firm rule

**The player can never hurt, punish or frighten Temutom.** There is no spray bottle and no shouting. The player can stroke, feed, play, lure, lift, wait and get out of the way. Every bad thing in this game happens to the player, and that is the joke.

## 10. Structure

- A **moment** is one round: one place, one chore or objective, 60 to 90 seconds.
- A **day** is five moments: Dawn, Morning, Afternoon, Evening and Night. Dawn is always 4:50am.
- A **week** is seven days and has one difficulty, which appears through the week and comes to a head in its final moment.

The weeks are the same seven as in the first game, seen from the other side:

| Week | The difficulty | What the player has to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Hoover** | Hoover the house. He regards this as an act of war. |
| 2 | **The Houseguest** | A visitor's hand reaches in to stroke him, badly. Protect the visitor. |
| 3 | **The Neighbour Cat** | He is worked up at the window and takes it out on whoever is nearest |
| 4 | **The Diet** | The vet has said less food. The player must refuse him. |
| 5 | **The Dog** | Looking after a friend's dog, in his house |
| 6 | **The Baby** | Two creatures who both need everything at once |
| 7 | **The Vet** | Get him into The Carrier |

## 11. Scoring and Standing

Each moment is scored out of 100:

- how much of the chore was done
- how long he was content
- how often his demands were understood first time
- how many plasters are left

Meeting the objective earns one star; higher scores earn two and three.

Stars raise the player's **Standing**, which is his opinion of them: **Intruder, Staff, Adequate Staff, Preferred Lap**, and finally **"Mine"**.

As Standing rises he gives slightly longer warnings, tolerates slightly more, and begins, without acknowledging it, to show affection: the head-butt, sleeping on the washing the player has just folded, a "gift" left on the Home screen.

## 12. Tone and voice

He narrates as before, now speaking to the player directly.

- On being fed promptly: *"Adequate."*
- On being stroked for too long: *"You have overstepped."*
- After biting: *"You did that to yourself."*
- After falling off the worktop: *"The worktop moved. You moved it."*

After every moment he gives a one-line review of the player, and after every day a review card, which can be shared.

> **You.** Breakfast was fourteen seconds late. I asked for the door and was offered a cuddle. You touched the stomach. You know what happens when you touch the stomach. **Must improve.**

## 13. Long-term layer

- **Standing,** as above.
- **Field notes:** a journal that fills in as the player observes him. Each entry records a behaviour and what it means ("Tail tip flicking: stop soon"), and the map of where he may be touched fills in zone by zone as each is tried. The notes are the player's own knowledge, written down.
- **Review archive:** every daily review card.
- **The cabinet:** every kind of object he has knocked off a surface.
- **He lives on the Home screen.** He may be asleep on the Settings button. The marks from the last moment are still on the glass.

## 14. Daily and live features

- **Today's Mood:** one moment a day, the same for everyone, with a twist ("woke up wrong", "it is raining and this is your fault"). His review is the shareable result.
- **A streak** for consecutive days.
- **Later:** optional reminders in his voice. *"It is 4:50."*

## 15. Monetisation

- None in the first version.
- Later: opt-in rewarded adverts for an extra plaster, a single purchase to remove adverts, and extra weeks.

## 16. Visual direction

- **Framing:** first person. The scene fills the screen; Temutom is large in it, often close enough to touch, sometimes too close.
- **Temutom:** as in the first game: large, round, very orange, small head, fixed contempt. Here he needs many more poses and a readable tail, ears and eyes, because they are the interface.
- **Claw marks** are drawn as bold cartoon slashes with a slight shadow, clearly an effect of the game, never a realistic imitation of a cracked or damaged screen.
- **The red** is one saturated colour that spreads from the bite, with no gore.
- **Interface:** small and plain, so that it is funny when he damages it.
- **Readability:** every mood is shown by at least two of tail, ears, eyes and sound, so that no single cue is essential.

## 17. Audio and haptics

Sound and vibration matter more here than in the first game, because they stand in for touch.

- A purr with a continuous soft rumble while he is content. The rumble stopping is the first sign of trouble.
- A low growl at the warning stage, with a faint uneven tremor.
- A rip and a sharp buzz for a swipe; a single hard thump for a bite.
- The scream, at length.
- A soft bump for the head-butt.
- Every sound has a visual equivalent, so the game is fully playable in silence. An optional "mood hints" setting shows a small gauge for players who find the body language hard to read.

## 18. The first 60 seconds

1. 4:50am. A dark kitchen and an empty bowl. Temutom is screaming. The screen shakes.
2. The player drags the pouch to the bowl. He eats. *"Adequate. Late, but adequate."*
3. He comes close. The player strokes his head; he purrs and the phone rumbles.
4. The player keeps stroking. The rumble stops. His tail lashes. He swipes, and four claw marks cross the screen and the interface. No plaster is lost this first time. *"You have overstepped."*
5. The moment ends with his first review.

Everything later is introduced the same way, alone, in a moment built around it.

## 19. Build scope

**First version:** Weeks 1 and 2 (70 moments), three places rising to five, five demands, the hidden Attention Band with full body language, claw marks, the bite, plasters, the escalation ladder, chores, the Brain Cell with three Schemes and four lapses, Standing, field notes, reviews, the cabinet and Today's Mood.

**Later:** Weeks 3 to 7, reminders, further Schemes, seasonal weeks.

## 20. Risks and open questions

- **Reading a hidden gauge.** The game fails if his mood cannot be read. The body language must be exaggerated and consistent, and needs testing with plain shapes before artwork.
- **Being punished may not be fun.** The humour has to land every time a plaster is lost. His lines, the fairness rule and instant restart are what make it a joke and not a penalty.
- **Flashing red.** A sudden full-screen red is a known risk for photosensitive players. The build specification sets limits on speed and repetition, and a softer setting.
- **Marks mistaken for damage.** Claw marks must look drawn, not real.
- **Animation cost.** This game needs about three times the cat poses of the first.
- **Two games, one cat.** If both are built they should share his artwork, voice and the model of his mood. If only one is built, this is the more distinctive of the two, and the one that shows better in a short video.
- **Decided:** this is a separate app, with the package name `temutom2` and the app identifier `com.temutom2.promethica`. The red wash is accepted as described. Claw marks do not build up over time. See the [build specification](../temutom2/build-specification.md).
- **Open:** should Temutom's appearance be based on the real cat? If so, reference photographs are needed.
- **Open:** "Living with Temutom" is a working title and has not been checked for existing use.

See the [build specification](../temutom2/build-specification.md).
