# Temutom: Staff Management – Concept Design

*16 November 2026 · Concept 10 of 11 · [Back to concept list](../game-concepts.md)*

All numbers in this document are proposed starting values, to be tuned through playtesting.

## 1. Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pitch** | You are an orange cat. The humans in your house are underperforming staff. Train them, correct them and take over the house, one room at a time. |
| **Genre** | Reverse management game with light real-time action |
| **Audience** | Cat owners and anyone who follows cat humour online; teens and adults |
| **Orientation** | Portrait, one thumb |
| **Session** | One "shift" of about 90 seconds; progress saves after every shift |
| **Works offline** | Yes |

## 2. The character

Temutom is an assertive, sometimes aggressive orange cat who believes to his core that people exist only to serve him. He is irritated by too little attention and equally irritated by too much. He is never wrong, and nothing is ever his fault.

He is also an orange cat in the contemporary sense: supremely confident, and only occasionally in possession of a working brain.

Every mechanic in the game comes from one of those traits:

| Trait | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| People exist to serve him | **Demands** and **staff training** (sections 4 and 5) |
| Irritated by too much or too little attention | The **Attention Band** (section 3) |
| Assertive, sometimes aggressive | Five blunt **verbs** and the **Outburst** (sections 4 and 6) |
| Orange | The **Brain Cell** (section 7) |
| Never his fault | The **narrator** and the **performance review** (section 11) |

## 3. Core mechanic: the Attention Band

A gauge at the bottom of the screen shows how much attention Temutom is receiving. On it sits a narrow band marking how much he currently wants. The band drifts up and down without warning.

- **Below the band:** he is being ignored. This is insubordination.
- **Above the band:** he is being handled. This is assault.
- **Inside the band:** acceptable, for now.

The player cannot set attention directly. It rises when staff look at him, talk to him, stroke him or pick him up, and falls when they are busy or absent. The player moves it by acting on the staff: screaming to get noticed, smacking a hand away, hiding under the bed.

Time spent outside the band fills his **Irritation** gauge (section 6).

## 4. Core gameplay

1. A shift begins in one room of the house, with one or two staff going about their day.
2. A thought bubble shows what Temutom wants: food, a door opened, a lap, a game.
3. The player uses his verbs to make the staff notice, understand and comply.
4. Meanwhile the player keeps attention inside the band.
5. The shift ends after about 90 seconds with a score, one to three stars and a written review of the staff.

**The five verbs**

| Verb | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Scream** | Everyone nearby notices. Blunt, and often misunderstood. | Wears on staff morale if overused |
| **Stare** | Slow and silent. Staff are more likely to work out what he wants. | Takes two seconds and can be missed |
| **Smack** | Stops stroking at once. Also corrects a wrong response. | Some morale |
| **Sit On** | Occupies a lap, a laptop or the clean washing. Rewards good service; blocks whatever the human was doing. | A little morale if he is in the way |
| **Knock Off** | Sends an object off a shelf. Nobody ignores it. | More morale, more if it breaks |

Controls are one-thumb: tap a place to go there, tap a person or object to choose a verb, and hold the large button at the bottom to scream.

## 5. Staff training

Each member of staff has a **Compliance** level for each kind of demand. It rises whenever they serve him correctly, rises faster if he rewards them straight afterwards (sitting on them, purring), and rises a little if he punishes a wrong response.

- **Low compliance:** they misread him. He screams for food and gets picked up.
- **Medium compliance:** they usually get it right when told.
- **High compliance:** they serve him before he asks. Breakfast appears at 4:50am.

Compliance is kept between shifts. Training the staff is the game's main sense of progress.

**Staff Morale** is the counterweight. Screaming, smacking, biting and breakages lower it. As it falls, staff start closing doors, then fetch the spray bottle, and at zero they fetch **The Carrier**, which ends the shift. Aggression is quick and effective, but it has to be rationed.

The two household staff differ:

| Staff member | Behaviour | Problem they cause |
|---|---|---|
| **The Soft One** | Adores him, strokes him unprompted, forgives almost anything | Too much attention; poor at understanding demands |
| **The Busy One** | Works from home, rarely looks up, understands him well | Too little attention; loses patience quickly |

## 6. Irritation and the Outburst

When Irritation is full, Temutom has an Outburst without asking the player: he bites the nearest hand or ankle, swipes the nearest ornament, or failing both, tears round the room.

An Outburst resets his Irritation and leaves him briefly content, but costs morale. The player can also trigger a smaller one deliberately. It is a tool, and an expensive one.

Biting is shown as cartoon slapstick: a yelp and a recoil, with no injury.

## 7. The orange twist: the Brain Cell

All orange cats share one brain cell, and it is rarely Temutom's turn.

A small indicator shows whether the Brain Cell is **elsewhere**, **on its way** or **his**.

- **When he has it** (a few seconds at a time): **Schemes** become available. These are short multi-step tricks, such as opening the treat cupboard himself or unplugging the hoover.
- **When he does not:** some of the player's instructions go wrong. He walks to the wrong place, gets into a box, or stares at a wall for two seconds.

Two rules keep this funny instead of frustrating:

- The lapses are short, never happen twice in quick succession and never decide a shift on their own.
- Staff who see a lapse find it endearing, and **morale goes up**. Being an idiot is what earns him forgiveness for everything else.

The skill is timing: save ambitious plans for when the Brain Cell arrives, and let the lapses repair the damage in between.

## 8. Thrones

Every room has one throne: the armchair, the laptop keyboard, the clean worktop, the pillow. Temutom claims it by sitting on it, undisturbed and in a tolerable mood, for long enough. Poorly trained staff will lift him off.

Claimed thrones are kept and shown on a map of the house. Claiming all of them is **Total Domestic Dominion**, the goal of the game.

## 9. Structure

- A **shift** is one round: one room, one objective, about 90 seconds.
- A **day** is five shifts: Dawn, Morning, Afternoon, Evening and Night.
- A **week** is seven days and has one threat to his rule, which appears through the week and is confronted in the final shift.

| Week | Threat | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Hoover** | Noise that drives Irritation up; hiding places |
| 2 | **The Houseguest Who "Isn't a Cat Person"** | A third human who gives no attention at all, and whose lap must therefore be taken |
| 3 | **The Neighbour Cat at the Window** | A rival; territory to defend |
| 4 | **The Diet** | Less food; the treat cupboard becomes essential |
| 5 | **The Dog** | A second animal competing for staff attention |
| 6 | **The Baby** | Staff permanently busy; new rules about what may be bitten |
| 7 | **The Vet** | The Carrier itself; everything learned is used to avoid it |

Shift objectives vary: get served a number of times, claim the room's throne, raise a staff member's compliance, survive a visit from the week's threat.

## 10. Scoring

Each shift is scored out of 100:

- how many of his demands were met
- how long attention stayed inside the band
- how much staff morale remains
- whether he is on a throne at the end

Meeting the objective earns one star; higher scores earn two and three. Stars raise his rank: **Nuisance, Menace, Despot, Tyrant, Emperor**.

## 11. Tone and voice

Temutom narrates in short, dry, imperious lines. He never admits fault and never notices his own lapses.

- On falling off a shelf: *"The shelf moved."*
- On being fed promptly: *"Adequate."*
- On being stroked for too long: *"You have overstepped."*
- On emerging from a box he was stuck in: *"I meant to do that. I will not be explaining why."*

After every shift he writes a one-line **performance review** of each member of staff. After every day he issues a full review card, which the player can share.

> **The Busy One.** Breakfast was fourteen seconds late. Door service remains poor. Lap was provided on request, but with sighing. **Must improve.**

The humour is dry, not crude. The narration needs no swearing.

## 12. Long-term layer

- **The house map**, filling with claimed thrones.
- **Staff files:** each staff member's compliance in each demand, shown as training certificates.
- **The cabinet:** a collection of every different object he has knocked off a surface, titled "Things That Were Too Close to the Edge".
- **Ranks**, earned from stars.
- **Review archive:** every daily review card he has issued.

## 13. Daily and live features

- **The Daily Grievance:** one shift a day, the same for everyone, with a twist ("the bowl is half empty", "bath night"). The result is shareable as his review of the day.
- **A streak** for consecutive days.
- **Seasonal weeks** later: The Christmas Tree, The Fireworks, The Suitcase.

## 14. Monetisation

- None in the first version.
- Later: opt-in rewarded adverts for a retry with morale restored, a single purchase to remove adverts, and extra weeks.

## 15. Visual direction

- **Framing:** one room fills the upper two-thirds of the screen, drawn as a cutaway seen from slightly above. Gauges and the Scream button sit in the lower third, within reach of the thumb.
- **Temutom:** large, round, very orange, with a small head and a fixed expression of contempt. His silhouette must be readable at a glance. When the Brain Cell is away, his eyes point in slightly different directions.
- **Staff:** drawn simply and seen mostly as hands, laps and ankles, which is how he sees them.
- **Style:** flat, boldly outlined 2D with warm domestic colours, so that the orange cat is always the brightest thing on screen.
- **Readability:** the Attention Band uses position and shape as well as colour. Each demand has its own icon.

## 16. Audio and haptics

- The scream is the signature sound: long, rising and indignant, lasting as long as the thumb is held.
- A deep, rumbling purr with a continuous soft vibration while attention is inside the band.
- A low growl that builds with Irritation.
- A hollow "bonk" and a slide whistle for lapses.
- A distinct crash for each kind of object knocked off.
- Every sound has a visual equivalent, so the game is fully playable in silence.

## 17. The first 60 seconds

1. The game opens in the kitchen at 4:50am. The bowl is empty. One large button pulses at the bottom of the screen.
2. The player holds it. Temutom screams. The Soft One shuffles in and fills the bowl. *"Adequate. Late, but adequate."*
3. The Soft One starts stroking him. The attention marker climbs above the band and a hand is highlighted. The player taps it: Smack. *"You have overstepped."*
4. The shift ends with his first review of the staff.

Each later verb and system is introduced the same way, alone, in a shift built around it.

## 18. Build scope

**First version:** Weeks 1 and 2 (70 shifts), three rooms rising to five, two staff plus the Houseguest, all five verbs, the Attention Band, Irritation, Compliance, Morale, the Brain Cell with three Schemes, thrones, reviews, the cabinet, ranks and the Daily Grievance.

**Later:** Weeks 3 to 7, seasonal weeks, further Schemes, leaderboards.

## 19. Risks and open questions

- **Several systems at once.** Attention, Irritation, Compliance, Morale and the Brain Cell are a lot for a one-thumb game. They must be introduced one at a time across the first week, and the screen must never show more than is in use.
- **Random lapses can feel unfair.** The rules in section 7 are the remedy, and need testing early.
- **Real-time play in short sessions.** The other concepts are mostly turn-based puzzles. This one depends on how it feels, and should be prototyped before artwork is commissioned.
- **Aggression and age rating.** Biting and smacking are central to the character. Keeping them slapstick should hold the lowest age rating, but this needs checking against store guidelines.
- **Existing games.** Games in which the player is a troublesome cat exist. The distinguishing features are the Attention Band, staff training and the Brain Cell.
- **Open:** should Temutom's appearance be based on the real cat? If so, reference photographs are needed.
- **Open:** "Temutom: Staff Management" is a working title and has not been checked for existing use.
